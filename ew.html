Chapter 1: Addiction

Q1. Explain Addiction (Nasha), give its types and causes.

Introduction:

Addiction, commonly known as Nasha, is a condition in which a person becomes physically or psychologically dependent on a substance or activity. The person may continue it even when it causes harm to their health, family, education, career, or social life.

Types of Addiction:

  1. Alcohol Addiction: Excessive and regular consumption of alcohol.
  2. Tobacco Addiction: Dependence on cigarettes, chewing tobacco, gutkha.
  3. Drug Addiction: Dependence on harmful substances like opioids or cocaine.
  4. Prescription Drug Addiction: Misuse of medicines like sleeping pills.
  5. Digital/Internet Addiction: Excessive use of mobile phones and gaming.
  6. Gambling Addiction: Uncontrollable desire to gamble despite losses.

Causes of Addiction:

  • Peer pressure: Friends encouraging substance use.
  • Stress and anxiety: Using substances to escape emotional problems.
  • Curiosity: Young people experimenting.
  • Family problems: Conflicts or lack of emotional support.
  • Easy availability: Easy access to harmful substances.

Conclusion:

Addiction affects the individual, family, and society. Awareness, family support, and timely treatment can help overcome it.

Q2. Symptoms of an addiction in an individual.

Addiction affects physical health, mental health, behaviour, and social relationships.

Symptoms:

  1. Strong craving: Intense desire to use the substance.
  2. Loss of control: Difficulty in reducing or stopping the behaviour.
  3. Increased tolerance: Needing more amounts for the same effect.
  4. Withdrawal symptoms: Anxiety, shaking, or sweating when stopped.
  5. Changes in behaviour: Becoming secretive, aggressive, or withdrawn.
  6. Neglect of responsibilities: Ignoring studies, work, or duties.
  7. Physical & Financial problems: Poor sleep, loss of appetite, and wasting money.

Q3. Short note on the impact of addiction on an individual.

Addiction seriously affects an individual's entire life:

  • Physical health: Weakness, poor nutrition, and damage to important organs.
  • Mental health: Increases stress, anxiety, depression, and irritability.
  • Education & Career: Loss of concentration, poor performance, and job loss.
  • Relationships & Finances: Family conflicts, mistrust, and heavy debts.
  • Social isolation: Avoiding family and friends, leading to loneliness.

Q4. Prevention strategies to overcome addiction.

  • Awareness & Education: Teaching society about the harmful effects of addictions.
  • Strong family support: Open communication and emotional backing.
  • Avoid peer pressure: Learning to confidently say "No" to bad habits.
  • Healthy lifestyle: Regular exercise, good sleep, and healthy food.
  • Counselling & Rehab: Seeking professional help and rehabilitation programmes.
  • Develop positive thinking: Staying optimistic and setting life goals.

Chapter 2: Positive Thinking

Q1. Distinguish between Positive and Negative Thinking.

Positive Thinking Negative Thinking
Focuses on solutions.Focuses mainly on problems.
Develops confidence and motivation.Creates self-doubt and fear.
Sees failure as a learning opportunity.Sees failure as the end of success.
Encourages taking constructive action.Often leads to avoidance.
Helps manage stress effectively.Can increase stress and anxiety.

Q2. Techniques to develop Positive Thinking.

  • Practice gratitude: Appreciate the good things in life.
  • Use positive self-talk: Replace negative thoughts with encouraging statements.
  • Set achievable goals: Small goals create motivation and confidence.
  • Learn from failures: Treat mistakes as learning opportunities.
  • Surround yourself with positive people: Supportive friends encourage healthy thinking.
  • Focus on solutions: Look for answers instead of crying over the problem.

Q3. Barriers to positive thinking.

The major barriers that stop a person from thinking positively are:

  • Fear of failure: Fear of making mistakes stops people from taking chances.
  • Low self-confidence: Underestimating one's own abilities.
  • Negative self-talk: Repeatedly thinking "I am not good enough."
  • Stress and anxiety: Stress makes it hard to focus on solutions.
  • Overthinking & Negative surroundings: Constantly worrying and staying with people who only criticize.
  • Comparison with others: Comparing life with others creates dissatisfaction.
Chapter 3: Women Entrepreneurship

Q1. Importance of women entrepreneurship and leadership.

Women entrepreneurship refers to businesses owned or managed by women. Its importance includes:

  • Economic & Rural development: Contributes to national income and supports rural growth.
  • Employment generation: Creates new job opportunities for society.
  • Financial independence: Helps women become self-reliant and confident.
  • Poverty reduction: Increased family income improves the standard of living.
  • Role models: Inspires younger generations of girls to start businesses.

Q2. Explain various sectors where women excel as entrepreneurs with a suitable industrial example.

Women are highly active in sectors such as:

  • Fashion, textiles, and jewellery.
  • Food and beverages (restaurants, catering).
  • Beauty, skincare, and wellness services.
  • Technology, IT, and E-commerce.
  • Education and skill-development coaching.

Note: Please refer to your own case study of women entrepreneurs for the industrial example.

Q3. Challenges and need for women entrepreneurs.

Major Challenges:

  • Lack of finance: Difficulty in getting business loans.
  • Family responsibilities: Balancing household duties with business.
  • Social barriers & Safety: Traditional mindsets and mobility concerns.
  • Lack of network & training: Limited access to mentors, technology, and business education.

Needs/Solutions:

  • Easier access to credit and loans.
  • Mentorship, training, and digital literacy programmes.
  • Awareness of helpful government schemes.
  • Family and community support.
Chapter 4: Journalism and Media

Q1. Role of Journalist and Media.

The media is the fourth pillar of democracy. Its key roles include:

  • Providing Information: Delivering accurate, unbiased, and timely news.
  • Public Awareness: Educating citizens about rights, health, and social issues.
  • Exposing Truth: Investigating and uncovering corruption and crimes.
  • Voice of the Public: Highlighting ordinary people's struggles to grab the government's attention.
  • Watchdog: Keeping a strict watch on the government and powerful organizations.

Q2. Educational Based Career in Journalism and Mass Media.

A career in journalism requires specific educational training:

  • BMM (Bachelor of Mass Media): Covers media, advertising, and journalism.
  • BA in Journalism and Mass Communication: Focused heavily on reporting and media ethics.
  • Diplomas & Master's Degrees: Short courses for specific skills or advanced degrees for investigative journalism.

Careers: News Reporter, Anchor, Content Writer, Editor, or PR Officer.

Q3. Skills Required for Career in Journalism and Media.

  • Strong Communication: Speaking and writing clearly and confidently.
  • Research Skills: Finding and verifying the exact truth before reporting.
  • Unbiased Attitude: Reporting neutrally without adding personal emotions.
  • Time Management: Working quickly to meet strict news deadlines.
  • Confidence & Tech-Savvy: Bravery to ask tough questions and basic knowledge of digital tools.
Chapter 5: Sustainable Resource Management

Q1. Types of Sustainable Natural Resources.

These are nature's gifts used to meet current needs without destroying them for future generations:

  • Renewable Resources: Never run out and replenish naturally (e.g., solar and wind energy).
  • Non-Renewable Resources: Take millions of years to form and must be used efficiently (e.g., coal, petroleum).
  • Biological Resources: Forests, plants, and wildlife saved through conservation.
  • Water Resources: Rivers and oceans sustained through rainwater harvesting and preventing pollution.

Q2. Methods of Sustainable Practices.

  • The 3Rs: Reduce, Reuse, and Recycle waste.
  • Renewable Energy: Switching to solar panels and wind turbines instead of coal.
  • Water Conservation: Fixing leaks, using drip irrigation, and harvesting rainwater.
  • Sustainable Agriculture: Farming with organic manure instead of harmful chemicals.
  • Afforestation: Planting trees to maintain oxygen balance and stop soil erosion.

Q3. Five Features and Importance of Sustainable Resource Management.

Five Features:

  1. Long-term planning for future needs.
  2. Minimal waste and pollution production.
  3. Using eco-friendly and green technology.
  4. Protecting animal habitats and ecosystems.
  5. Balancing human lifestyle needs with environmental health.

Importance:

It saves crucial resources (water, clean air) for the future, controls global warming by reducing emissions, prevents plant and animal extinction, and ensures steady economic growth.
